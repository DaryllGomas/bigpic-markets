# Eagle Eye W30 — Commodities & Forex (Agent 3)

*Point-in-time snapshot as of Sunday 2026-07-26. Covers the trading week ending Friday 2026-07-24 plus breaking weekend news through Sun 07-26.*

**BOTTOM LINE:** The dominant story of the week was oil. A sharp re-escalation of the **2026 Iran war** (ongoing since late Feb 2026 — Sun 07-26 was "Day 149") pushed Brent above $100 intraweek on Strait of Hormuz supply fears, before a **weekend de-escalation** (first U.S. strike pause in ~2 weeks + Iran-Oman/Pakistan-China diplomacy) sent crude gapping sharply lower on the Sunday reopen. Everything else — a 40-year-low yen, record-high copper, a firm dollar, EM currencies at record lows — is downstream of the oil/geopolitics complex and the resulting "higher-for-longer" rate repricing.

## Energy

### Crude Oil — the geopolitical premium and its unwind

The week was defined by a war-risk premium building and then partially collapsing.

- **WTI (front-month):** ~**$90/bbl** at Friday 2026-07-24 close (Trading Economics prints $90.47, -1.87% on the day; Fox's weekend note implies a ~$89.3 Friday reference — treat as ~$89–90). **Up roughly +10% on the week.**
- **Brent:** ~**$96.8–$97/bbl** at Friday 2026-07-24 close, **down ~4% on the day** but **up ~10–12% on the week** (Bloomberg/Fortune/Trading Economics). Brent traded **above $100 intraweek** at the peak of the escalation.

**What drove it (all inside the 07-18→07-26 window):**
- Monday 07-20: Oil jumped after President Trump said Iran "would pay" for the deaths of three U.S. service members, and Houthi forces declared a maritime embargo on Saudi Arabia (CNBC, 2026-07-20).
- The U.S. (CENTCOM) bombed Iran for **13 consecutive nights** through mid/late week, targeting military and maritime infrastructure; strait transits collapsed (see Freight below).
- Friday 07-24 pullback: reports that **Pakistan, with Chinese backing, was seeking to revive U.S.–Iran negotiations** eased fears of a prolonged Hormuz closure, knocking Brent down ~4% intraday.

**Fundamental backdrop is bearish — the spike is pure geopolitics.** OPEC+ is adding **+548,000 bpd in August**, U.S./non-OPEC supply growth is running ~3x demand growth, and OPEC's own latest outlook flipped to a **balanced-to-oversupplied 2026** (some sell-side desks model a ~2.0 mb/d surplus and Brent/WTI 2026 averages back near $56/$52 absent the war premium). This gap between weak fundamentals and a war-driven spot price is exactly why crude is so headline-sensitive right now.

**EIA inventories (released 2026-07-22, week ending 07-17):** U.S. commercial crude **+2.0 million barrels to 411.7 million** — builds "across the board" in crude and products, yet still **~6% below the five-year average** for the date. So the physical U.S. market is loosening even as the geopolitical bid dominates screen prices.

### Natural Gas — Henry Hub soft on ample supply

- Henry Hub front-month ~**$2.88/MMBtu** on Friday 2026-07-24 (-1.16% on the day); prompt-month settled ~**$2.91** on 07-22 (Trading Economics / AGA). *(Note: one AGA snapshot referenced a $3.15–$3.34 range earlier in the month; the specific 07-22→07-24 prints cluster below $3, so I weight the sub-$3 figure.)*
- Supply-heavy backdrop: storage **~6.4% above the five-year seasonal average** (as of 07-17) and Lower-48 production averaging **~110.4 bcf/d** in July. Gas is notably decoupled from the oil rally — a domestic, supply-driven market largely immune to the Hormuz premium.

### Uranium / Nuclear Fuel — steady in the mid-$80s (nuclear thesis)

- U3O8 spot **~$85.84/lb as of 2026-07-23** (metalcharts, spot proxy — *not a Friday settle*), roughly flat on the day. Sits within the nuclear thesis's expected band (thesis flagged a ~$100 Jan-2026 spot peak with long-term contracts $75–85). No fresh fuel-cycle catalyst inside the window; the structural 30–40M lb/yr deficit thesis (CCJ, LEU, UUUU, URA/SRUUF) is unchanged. Elevated oil/energy prices are a modest tailwind to the "power scarcity" narrative underpinning the nuclear complex.

## Metals

### Gold — capped below $4,100 by "higher-for-longer"

- **Spot gold ~$4,055.82/oz** at Friday 2026-07-24 (+$6.90 / +0.17% on the day); **up ~+0.96% on the week** — a modest gain despite an active war. Aug futures ~$4,053.
- The war bid was offset by a **hawkish rate repricing**: higher oil → energy-driven inflation fears → markets pricing rates staying higher for longer (reporting cited ~80% odds of a *September hike*, a sharp shift from the rate-cut path priced earlier in 2026). Non-yielding gold struggles when hike odds rise. **FOMC is next week (07-28/29)** — Fed funds held at 3.50–3.75%, decision widely expected to stay unchanged; that meeting is the near-term swing factor for gold and the dollar.

### Silver — outperforming, ratio compressing

- **Spot silver ~$58.40/oz** at Friday 2026-07-24 (+$1.13 / +1.98% on the day); **up ~+2% on the week**, outpacing gold and compressing the **gold/silver ratio to ~69.5** (USAGOLD). (Silver's early-2026 record highs are historical context, not this week.)

### Copper — near record highs; Dr. Copper bullish (critical-minerals thesis)

- **LME copper ~$13,626.50/t** on Friday 2026-07-24, **+0.7% on the week** (Business Recorder); Trading Economics prints ~$13,850/t — either way, **near historic highs** and at/above the top of the critical-minerals thesis's $11,000–13,500/t BoA target band.
- Bullish micro: **Shanghai (SHFE) copper inventories fell 12.9% w/w to 69,610 t — lowest since Feb 2024**, tight ex-U.S. stocks, resilient electrification/AI-datacenter demand. The oil spike above $100 added a broad-commodity/inflation bid. Dr. Copper is flashing tight-supply strength, consistent with the structural 2026 copper deficit thesis (FCX, SCCO).

### Supply-Chain / Freight — Hormuz chaos vs. soft dry-bulk

- **Baltic Dry Index ~2,671** mid-month (a >2-week low, down from ~2,944 on 07-11) — dry-bulk *weak*, signaling soft raw-material shipping demand, disconnected from the oil/tanker story.
- **Tanker & war-risk explosion around Hormuz:** war-risk insurance premiums surged to **3–10% of hull value (from ~0.25% pre-war)**; Hormuz transits fell to **10 on 07-21 (from 16 on 07-20)**; Persian Gulf→China (270kt) assessed at **$77.96/t on 07-22**. (VLCC benchmark day-rates were quoted ~$14,800/day / -44% m/m as of 07-24 — the rate signal is noisy given reduced, insurance-distorted crossings.) Bab el-Mandeb/Red Sea also disrupted. This is the physical transmission channel of the geopolitical premium into oil and EM fuel-importer stress.

## Weekend Developments

**A material weekend event occurred — and it was a de-escalation, not a new shock.** This is the key thing to carry into Monday's open.

- **Sat 2026-07-25 (CNN live):** The U.S. military did **not** announce new strikes on Iran — **the first night without an announced strike in ~2 weeks.**
- **Sun 2026-07-26 (Fox live / globalsecurity "Day 149"):** U.S. strikes officially **"on a hold"** as diplomacy proceeds. **Iran-Oman technical/political talks** over Strait of Hormuz control were described by Tehran as "productive," though sources flagged "more skepticism than optimism." Pakistan/China-brokered U.S.–Iran contact continued. Crucially, **the U.S. naval blockade of Iran remained fully operational** (CENTCOM posted resupply imagery) — so the strait is not cleared, the shooting has merely paused.
- **Oil reaction on the Sunday reopen (as of 2026-07-26, weekend futures proxy — NOT a Friday close):** **Brent -$5.58 (-5.8%) to ~$91.20**; **WTI -$4.91 (-5.5%) to ~$84.40** (Fox), unwinding much of the week's war premium on diplomacy hopes. Expect crude to open the new week sharply lower if the pause holds.
- **Residual risk still live:** tanker incidents continued in the **Red Sea**; Iran alleged a **Ukrainian attack on a commercial vessel in the Caspian Sea** (one sailor killed); Israel conducted strikes in **Gaza**. War-risk insurance and reduced Hormuz transits mean the physical supply overhang can snap back on any renewed escalation.

**Net:** Oil enters the week with a fragile, headline-dependent -5-6% weekend gap lower. A holding ceasefire/diplomacy = crude retraces toward its bearish ($56–70) fundamental gravity; a breakdown of talks or a fresh strait incident = instant re-spike above $100. Watch the FOMC (07-28/29) alongside the Iran headlines — the two together drive gold, the dollar, and the yen.
