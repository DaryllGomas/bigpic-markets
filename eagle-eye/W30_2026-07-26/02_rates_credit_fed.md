# Agent 2 — Rates, Credit & Fed

*Point-in-time snapshot as of Sunday 2026-07-26. Covers the trading week ending Friday 2026-07-24 plus weekend breaking news (Sat 07-25 / Sun 07-26). Next FOMC: Tue-Wed July 28-29, 2026 (decision Wed 07-29, 2:00 PM ET; Chair Warsh presser 2:30 PM ET).*

**Headline:** The rates regime has flipped. A hot US-Iran war pushed Brent above $100/bbl this week, and fed funds futures now price a meaningful (~35-38%) chance of a rate HIKE at the July 29 meeting — a dramatic reversal from the "3 cuts by year-end" consensus that prevailed earlier in 2026 (per our own Feb-2026 calendar note). Treasuries sold off across the curve on inflation fears. Then, over the weekend, US and Iran reportedly paused attacks — the key breaking de-escalation into the FOMC.

---

## Yield Curve

The curve remained modestly upward-sloping and was little changed in shape despite the yield surge — the sell-off was a fairly parallel, inflation-driven bear move rather than a steepening/flattening event.

- **2s10s:** ~+36 bps on Fri 07-24 (4.69% − 4.33%) vs ~+37 bps on Fri 07-17 (4.55% − 4.18%) — essentially flat/1 bp flatter on the week; still positively sloped.
- **3m10y:** ~+79 bps on Fri 07-24 (4.69% − 3.90%) — clearly positive (dis-inverted), no recession signal from this spread.
- The front end (2Y +15 bps) rose slightly more than the belly/long end, a touch of bear-flattening consistent with the market pulling forward hawkish Fed risk.

## Fed Watch

**This was a pre-FOMC blackout week (blackout runs July 18-30, 2026), so there were NO Fed speakers — the calendar was quiet by design.** The July 28-29 meeting will be Chair Kevin Warsh's second meeting (he was sworn in as the 17th Fed Chair on May 22, 2026).

**Fed funds pricing into July 29 (current target 3.50-3.75%):**
- **Hold at 3.50-3.75%:** ~61-65%
- **Hike +25 bps to 3.75-4.00%:** ~35-38% (this is the story)
- **Cut:** effectively ~0%; a 50 bp move is priced at ~0

The hike odds have exploded: CME FedWatch showed just **10.7% on July 15**, rising to **34.7% by July 22**, and **~38% by July 24** — a more-than-tripling in a week, driven by oil topping $100 amid the escalating US-Iran conflict. Most economists still expect a hold, but the debate is now purely hold-vs-hike, not hold-vs-cut.

**Context (historical, dated):**
- At Warsh's first meeting (June 16-17, 2026) the FOMC held unanimously at 3.50-3.75% but signaled in its projections a potential hike later in 2026 to counter the Iran-war inflation spike. Warsh shortened the statement to ~130 words and dropped forward guidance ("regime change in a velvet glove"). He has argued AI will ultimately be disinflationary via productivity.
- **June FOMC minutes** were released July 8, 2026 (BEFORE this week's window) and showed officials split — some saw inflation easing enough to cut, others saw hikes. No minutes were released this week.
- **SLOOS:** No new Senior Loan Officer survey this week. The most recent is the April 2026 SLOOS (released May 2026); the next quarterly reading lands around the late-July/August FOMC cycle.

## Credit Markets

Credit was strikingly calm given the geopolitical shock — spreads widened only modestly and remain historically tight, signaling no funding/credit stress despite the equity and rates volatility.

- **HY OAS (ICE BofA US High Yield):** ~**277 bps (2.77%)** as of July 23, 2026, up from ~269 bps on July 10 — a modest ~8 bp widening. Still very tight by historical standards.
- **IG OAS:** ~80 bps as of July 2026; BBB ~100 bps; AA ~50 bps.
- **HY-IG differential:** ~195-200 bps.
- Takeaway: The war/oil shock is being expressed almost entirely through rates and inflation breakevens, not through credit risk premia. If Middle East escalation resumed and pushed oil sharply higher, HY is the spot most vulnerable to a repricing — but as of Friday it was orderly.

## Liquidity

System liquidity tightened further this week. Per the Fed H.4.1 released July 23, 2026 (week ended July 22):

- **Bank reserves:** ~**$3.06 trillion** ($3,062,149M), **down ~$80.6B on the week**.
- **ON RRP:** ~**$352.7 billion** ($352,703M), up ~$4.9B on the week.
- **Total Fed balance sheet:** ~$6.75 trillion (QT ongoing).
- **TGA (Treasury General Account):** ~**$835.4 billion** as of July 22 (vs ~$333.5B a year earlier).

Following the early-July debt-limit increase, Treasury has been rapidly rebuilding the TGA toward a ~$1 trillion target (±$50B) for late July, largely via a surge in net bill issuance. Because every dollar into the TGA drains reserves ~1-for-1, the combination of TGA rebuild + ongoing QT has pushed system liquidity (reserves + ON RRP) to its lowest since runoff began, and repo-market rate pressures have intensified. This is a background tightening the desk/Fed is watching, and a potential factor in when QT ends.

## Weekend Developments

**Yes — a material geopolitical de-escalation, which is the single most important input into the July 29 FOMC.**

- **Sun 2026-07-26:** Reports indicate the **US and Iran paused their attacks** after days of escalation — airstrikes halted and talks progressing (developing/fluid as of this writing). If it holds, this should relieve the oil/inflation impulse that drove hike odds up, and could pull rates and hike probability back down into the meeting. Treat as preliminary.
- **Thu 2026-07-23 (in-window):** Brent crude crossed **$100/bbl** (highest since May 26, ~+29% over the prior month) after Houthi missile/drone strikes hit Saudi tankers in the Red Sea and the US-Iran ceasefire collapsed. This was the proximate trigger for the week's yield spike and the surge in Fed hike odds.
- **Effective 12:01 AM EDT Fri 2026-07-17 (window edge):** The US Treasury revoked its temporary waiver allowing Iranian oil sales (previously authorized through Aug 21); transactions were no longer permitted, tightening the supply picture.
- **Historical context (dated, NOT this week):** The US first struck Iran ~July 8, 2026, which began the oil surge and the 2026 Iran war / Strait of Hormuz crisis. Commercial traffic through the Strait had fallen >90% since the conflict began.
- No Fed policy actions, Treasury refunding announcements (Q3 refunding is due early August), or other rates-specific official communications occurred over the weekend (Fed remains in blackout through July 30).
