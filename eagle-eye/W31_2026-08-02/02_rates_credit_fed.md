# Agent 2 — Rates, Credit & Fed

*Point-in-time snapshot as of Sunday 2026-08-02. Covers the trading week Mon 2026-07-27 through Fri 2026-07-31 plus breaking news from Friday's 4pm ET close through Sunday 2026-08-02.*

**Headline: the Fed blinked, and the bond market punished it.** The FOMC held at 3.50-3.75% on Wednesday 2026-07-29 in a **9-3 vote with three hawkish dissents** — the first time since September 2016 that three officials dissented in the same direction. Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference was read as vague, and the long end revolted: the **30-year yield tore through 5.20% to a 19-year high (~5.25%)** while the **2-year FELL 5 bps**. That is a textbook credibility bear-steepener — BofA labeled it a "central bank inflation credibility shock." 2s10s steepened ~11 bps to ~+47 bps in one week. JPMorgan pulled its next-hike call forward from H2-2027 to **December 2026**.

**Then the weekend flipped the input.** On Saturday 2026-08-01 Trump called off a planned strike on Iran, and on Sunday 2026-08-02 said talks begin Monday with a framework centered on the "Immediate, Complete, and Total OPENING OF THE HORMUZ STRAIT." Brent fell as much as 7.3%. OPEC+ separately agreed a September quota hike. The oil-supply-shock leg that has been driving hike pricing all month is suddenly at risk of unwinding — the single most important thing to watch at Monday's open.

**What drove it:**
- **The hold plus a vague presser** was read as the Fed declining to defend the 2% target with action. Yields rose again Friday 2026-07-31 as the three dissenters published statements arguing rates should be higher.
- **Inflation data was cooperative but not enough.** June PCE (released Thu 2026-07-30) showed headline down to 3.7% y/y from 4.1% and **core PCE at 3.3% y/y** from 3.4%, with core +0.1% m/m (cooler than the 0.2% consensus). Still 130 bps above target after five-plus years of overshoot.
- **Fiscal/supply and term premium.** The long end carries the Treasury borrowing burden plus the AI-capex borrowing wave. Quarterly refunding lands next week.
- **Global spillover.** The selloff was not US-only: Japan's 30Y hit 4% and the UK 10Y reached 5.14% during the July 2026 rout.
- Mortgage read-through: Freddie Mac 30-year fixed at **6.66%**, unchanged w/w and still the highest since August 2025.

## Yield Curve

**Decisively steeper — and steepening for the wrong reason.** BofA's framing (via Fortune, 2026-08-01) is the right lens: a *flattening* curve after a hold would say markets trust the Fed to deliver; a *steepening* curve says markets think the Fed is behind the curve and will let inflation run. We got the latter.

| Spread | Fri 2026-07-31 | Prior Fri 2026-07-24 | Change | Read |
|--------|----------------|----------------------|--------|------|
| **2s10s** | **+47 bps** | +36 bps | **+11 bps steeper** | Bear steepening; credibility discount |
| **2s30s** | ~+97 bps | +83 bps | **~+14 bps steeper** | Long end doing the repricing |
| **5s30s** | +83 bps *(07-30)* | +72 bps | **+11 bps steeper** | Term premium rebuild |
| **3m10y** | ~+93 bps *(3M as of 07-30)* | +79 bps | **~+14 bps steeper** | Firmly positive — no recession signal |

- No part of the curve is inverted. 3m10y at roughly +90 bps is the widest it has been in this cycle's recent run — the classic recession indicator is silent.
- The steepening is **bear steepening** (long yields up, short yields down), which is the more inflationary/fiscal-risk variant, not the "Fed is about to cut" bull steepener.
- Practical implication: duration is where the pain is. Anything discounted off the 30Y — nuclear and grid-storage project finance, space and quantum long-dated cash flows, data-center capex — is being repriced by term premium, not by the policy rate.

**Statement language (verbatim, Fed press release 2026-07-29):**
> "Inflation remains elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent goal, in part reflecting supply shocks that have driven price increases in certain sectors, including energy."

The statement also described activity as "expanding at a solid pace despite elevated uncertainty that owes, in part, to the conflict in the Middle East," with strong productivity growth and capital investment, and job gains keeping pace with the workforce.

**Warsh press conference (2026-07-29) — verbatim:**
> "Let me reiterate: There is no soft inflation target. There is no soft implicit target, not on this committee's watch."

> "We have begun a new chapter, and we understand that the five-plus years of inflation above target cannot be cured in nine weeks, or by a single month of modest price decreases."

> "We are not relying on any one individual piece of data as cover or as an excuse, or as validation. What I care about is trends on the data."

The market did not buy it. Warsh has stripped forward guidance out of Fed communication (he shortened the June statement to ~130 words and has said the Fed under him "isn't in the forecasting business"). Investors read hawkish words with no mechanism as talk without walk. Apollo's Torsten Slok: abandoning forward guidance without explaining *how* the Fed gets to target risks a steeper curve and investor skepticism.

**The dissenters' statements (published Fri 2026-07-31):**

| Official | Quote |
|----------|-------|

| **Kashkari** | "In my view, a potential series of small policy moves would be better than waiting and eventually concluding that even bolder actions were necessary." |

**Credit barely flinched.** The entire shock was expressed in rates and term premium, not in credit risk premia. Spreads widened a few basis points around the FOMC and then partially retraced.

| **ICE BofA US High Yield OAS** | **284 bps** | 287 bps (week high) | 279 bps | 277 bps | **+5 bps** vs 07-24 |
| **ICE BofA US Corporate (IG) OAS** | **80 bps** | 81 bps | 80 bps | 79 bps | **flat** |
| HY − IG differential | ~204 bps | ~206 bps | ~199 bps | ~198 bps | ~+5 bps |

*Friday 2026-07-31 OAS values were not yet published as of this writing.*

- HY OAS peaked at **287 bps on FOMC day (Wed 07-29)** and eased to 284 bps by Thursday. Both HY and IG remain **historically very tight** — HY sits far below the ~800 bp level associated with recession stress.
- The signal: **no funding or default stress.** Credit is treating the Fed's inflation problem as a nominal-growth-friendly outcome, not a solvency problem. Corporate borrowers are being repriced through the risk-free curve, not the spread.
- The vulnerability: at 284 bps there is almost no compensation for default risk. HY is the asset with the least cushion if either (a) the Fed actually hikes into a slowing economy, or (b) the long-end selloff continues and refinancing math breaks. Watch HY OAS through 300 bps as the first genuine crack.
- Equity vol repriced more than credit did — VIX rose to 20.66 from 18.65 over the comparable stretch while HY moved only ~15 bps. That gap is worth watching.

## Liquidity

**Reserves fell below $3 trillion for the first time in this cycle.** Per the Fed H.4.1 released Thu 2026-07-30 (week ended Wed 2026-07-29):

| Item | Week ended 2026-07-29 | W/W change |
|------|-----------------------|------------|
| **Reserve balances** | **$2,984,570M (~$2.98T)** | **−$77,579M** |
| **Treasury General Account (TGA)** | **$910,776M (~$911B)** | **+$81,153M** |
| Reverse repos — **total** | $337,051M | −$9,297M |
| — Foreign official & international accounts | $334,475M | −$10,283M |
| — **Others (domestic ON RRP facility)** | **$2,576M** | +$986M |
| Repurchase agreements (Standing Repo) | **$3M** | ~0 |
| Total assets | $6,738,190M (~$6.74T) | −$9,188M |
| — US Treasury securities | $4,519,654M | +$3,993M |
| — MBS | $1,930,855M | −$13,933M |

**Two corrections to how this is usually reported, both material:**

1. **The domestic ON RRP facility is empty — $2.6 billion.** The $337B "reverse repo" headline is ~99% the *foreign official repo pool*, not the domestic facility. The ON RRP buffer that absorbed QT from 2022-2024 is gone. There is no shock absorber left between Treasury issuance and bank reserves.

2. **QT is over and has been since late 2025.** The Fed ended balance-sheet runoff in **October/November 2025** after a ~$2.4T reduction, then ran Reserve Management Purchases of roughly **$40B/month in T-bills from December 2025 through mid-April 2026**. Today's balance sheet is roughly flat: MBS runs off passively (−$13.9B this week) and is largely replaced with Treasuries. So **reserves are draining because of the TGA rebuild, not because of QT.**

**The mechanism that matters:** TGA rose $81B in one week and reserves fell $78B — very nearly one-for-one, exactly as the plumbing predicts. Following the early-July 2026 debt-limit increase, Treasury has been rebuilding cash toward a ~$1 trillion target via heavy net bill issuance, and it is now at $911B. Each dollar into the TGA drains a dollar of reserves. With ON RRP at zero, there is nothing to cushion it.

**Warsh's balance-sheet review:** in June 2026 he formed independent task forces to "review the benefits and risks of the current ample reserves regime and the composition of the balance sheet" — i.e. whether to move toward scarce reserves and a leaner balance sheet paired with a permanent standing repo backstop. The July statement retained the "maintaining ample reserves" line, so no change yet. Standing Repo usage of **$3 million** says there is no acute funding stress today — but reserves under $3T with an empty ON RRP is the zone where SOFR/IORB pressure has historically appeared (it last did in Q4 2025). **This is the quiet risk in the system.**
