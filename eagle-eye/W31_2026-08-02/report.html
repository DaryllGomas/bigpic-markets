This Week’s Take

Executive Summary

Ⅰ The Weekend Rewrote the Trade

On Saturday 2026-08-01, Trump cancelled a planned strike on Iran, saying the “perimeters of a deal” had been agreed — a framework centered on the “Immediate, Complete, and Total OPENING OF THE HORMUZ STRAIT” and an end to Iran’s nuclear threat. Talks begin Monday 2026-08-03. Crude gapped down hard on the Sunday electronic open: Brent traded as low as $81.55 against Friday’s October settle of $87.93, compounded by OPEC+ approving another +188 kb/d for September — the sixth straight monthly increase, completing the full rollback of the 1.65 mb/d voluntary cuts. US equity futures opened risk-on. This is the dominant input for Monday, and it points directly against everything the market spent last week pricing.

🕊️ The biggest driver into the Aug 3 open — but the strait is still closed on Day 155, and Iran denied the agreement the same day it was announced.

Ⅱ Because Last Week Was a Hawkish Shock

The FOMC held at 3.50–3.75% on Wednesday 2026-07-29, but on a 9–3 vote with Hammack, Kashkari and Logan all dissenting in favor of a 25bp hike — the first triple same-direction dissent since September 2016. New Chair Kevin Warsh’s press conference was read as tough talk without a mechanism, and the bond market called the bluff: the 2-year fell 5bp to 4.28% while the 30-year rose 9bp to ~5.25%, a 19-year high. That is a textbook credibility bear-steepener — 2s10s widened 11bp to +47bp. BofA labeled it a “central bank inflation credibility shock”; JPMorgan pulled its next-hike call forward from H2-2027 all the way to December 2026. The market now prices a hike, not a cut, at every horizon through year-end: ~63–65% odds for September, 82.7% cumulative odds of a higher funds rate by December.

“There is no soft inflation target. There is no soft implicit target, not on this committee’s watch.”— Chair Kevin Warsh, 2026-07-29
🏛️ The front end rallied into three hike dissents. The long end did all the repricing — that is a credibility discount, not a policy-rate story.

Ⅲ The Tension Into Monday Is the Whole Story

The September-hike case has rested almost entirely on an oil-driven supply shock. If Hormuz genuinely reopens, that leg collapses — argue for lower hike odds, a lower long end, and a partial unwind of the bear steepener. But core PCE at 3.3% is not an oil story; it is five-plus years of embedded overshoot, and that is what the three dissenters were actually arguing about. A dovish oil headline does not fix a credibility problem. Expect a knee-jerk long-end rally that the dissenters’ logic will fight. And fade the Hormuz headline itself: this is at minimum the third announced reopening of 2026 (April ceasefire, the June 17 Islamabad MOU, now August), the strait is still closed on Day 155, Iran publicly denied any reopening agreement the same day Trump announced it, and tankers were still being struck through Saturday — including the Qatari-laden LNG carrier Gaslog Shanghai, disabled by a projectile to its engine room.

🧭 Two forces pulling opposite ways into one open — a disinflationary oil gap against a Fed that just drew three hike dissents over a problem oil didn’t create.

Ⅳ Beneath the Macro — a Semis Rout and an Earnings-Quality Screen

Two structural stories deserve more attention than the +1.05% weekly SPX print suggests. First, the SOX fell 21% in July — its worst month since October 2008, erasing $2.2 trillion, triggered by a 07-27/28 report that China had begun mass-producing immersion DUV lithography tools (ASML −8%). Korea’s Kospi ended July −22% with four circuit breakers, then ripped a record +18% on Friday. Second, earnings dispersion has become the cleanest signal in the tape: the market is no longer paying for AI capex, it is paying for AI capex that converts to revenue. MSFT raised spend and gained ~$490B in a single session — the largest one-day market-cap gain in history — because Azure accelerated to +43%. META raised its capex floor to $130B and lost 8% because free cash flow collapsed to $784M. Vertiv (VRT) beat and raised and still lost a sixth of its value on a timing-related revenue miss.

📈 A discrimination regime, not a de-rating — and VRT’s −16.8% says the “picks-and-shovels are safer than the chips” premise took a direct hit. PLTR Monday is the next test.

But at 284 bp there is almost no compensation for default risk — watch HY OAS through 300 bp as the first genuine crack. On the plumbing side, H.4.1 (week ended 07-29) shows reserve balances at $2.985T, −$78B, against a TGA at $911B, +$81B — nearly one-for-one, exactly as the mechanics predict, as Treasury rebuilds cash toward ~$1T following the early-July debt-limit increase.

Two corrections to how this is usually reported:
1. The domestic ON RRP facility is empty — $2.6B. The $337B “reverse repo” headline is ~99% the foreign official repo pool. The buffer that absorbed QT from 2022–2024 is gone; there is no shock absorber between Treasury issuance and bank reserves.
2. QT ended in October/November 2025 after a ~$2.4T reduction. Reserves are draining from the TGA rebuild, not from runoff.
Standing Repo usage of $3 million says no acute stress today — but sub-$3T reserves with an empty ON RRP is the zone where SOFR/IORB pressure has historically appeared. This is the quiet risk in the system.

The speaker slate is unusually thin: only Cook (Wed), Musalem (Thu) and Barkin (Fri), all tagged Low impact, and none of them among the three dissenters. There is no scheduled opportunity for Hammack, Kashkari or Logan to press their case, and no Warsh appearance to clarify a press conference the market found baffling — the credibility question raised on 07-29 stays open all week. Two mechanical rates events do matter: Treasury’s quarterly borrowing estimates Monday and the Quarterly Refunding Statement Wednesday size the coupon supply that is driving the term-premium rebuild. July FOMC minutes (~08-19) and Jackson Hole (08-27 to 08-29, where Warsh keynotes) are the next real communication events.

Monday, August 3 — The Most Important Day for Narrative, and It Has Nothing to Do with the Data

US–Iran talks begin Monday afternoon — the market will trade the headlines, not the ISM. Watch the transit counts, not the announcements: this is the third announced Hormuz reopening of 2026 and the strait is still closed on Day 155. ISM Manufacturing (10:00) is the only High-impact release, and its prices-paid subindex matters more than the headline given three Fed officials just dissented over inflation. Two under-the-radar catalysts land the same day: the July SLOOS at 2:00 PM — the first read on whether the war/oil shock and a 19-year-high 30Y are tightening bank credit standards, a genuine catalyst rather than a formality given how tight HY sits at 284bp — and Treasury’s quarterly marketable borrowing estimates, which size the supply that has been driving term premium. Japan’s Katayama is also expected to confirm the joint US–Japan yen action. PLTR and AMD report after the close; PLTR options price a ~15% move and it is the most important single thesis event of the week.

Tuesday, August 4 — SpaceX Reports Its First-Ever Public Earnings

SpaceX (SPCX) reports its first-ever public earnings — a thesis-level event for the space book. The stock closed at $108.37, below its $135 IPO price and more than 52% off its post-IPO high, with Friday-expiry IV at 163% and options implying a 14–15% move. Whatever it prints will reset how the market values the entire private-space complex. On the macro side, JOLTS (10:00) is the week’s first real labor datapoint ahead of Friday’s payrolls, and it carries extra weight because the hike debate now hinges on whether the labor market is loose enough to absorb an oil-driven price shock. Trade Balance and Factory Orders are second-order.

Wednesday, August 5 — The Quarterly Refunding Statement Is the Real Rates Event

Treasury’s Quarterly Refunding Statement is the day’s most consequential item for rates — coupon auction sizes are the direct mechanism behind the term-premium rebuild that took the 30Y to a 19-year high. JPMorgan expects no changes ahead of the midterms; a surprise increase would be a genuine problem for duration. ADP (8:15) and ISM Services (10:00) are the payroll appetizers. Governor Lisa Cook speaks at 4:05 PM in Anchorage — the first Board speaker since the blackout lifted, and the first chance to hear whether the dissenters’ argument has moved anyone in the center. Also ALB and SYM report (critical-minerals and robotics theses respectively).

Thursday, August 6 — A Quieter Tape with Two Thesis-Specific Landmines

SPCX’s lockup expires, releasing 911.5M pre-IPO shares — two days after its first earnings print, which is an unusually punishing sequence for a stock already below its IPO price. Jobless claims (8:30) and prelim unit labor costs matter more than usual: unit labor costs feed directly into the inflation-persistence argument the three dissenters are making. H.4.1 at 4:30 PM shows whether the TGA rebuild keeps draining reserves below $2.985T with the ON RRP buffer at zero. Musalem speaks at 5:30 PM.

Friday, August 7 — Payrolls Is the Week’s Main Event

Consensus is ~87.5K against 57K prior, with unemployment expected to tick up to 4.3% from 4.2%. The setup is genuinely two-sided in a way it has not been for months: a hot print with firm average hourly earnings hands the September hike to the dissenters, while a weak one collides with a Fed that has explicitly said it will not treat labor softness as license to ease while inflation runs at 3.3%. Note the asymmetry — there is no CPI or PCE this week, so payrolls carries the entire data burden for September positioning. VST reports into a 52-week low. Barkin speaks at 10:00. This is also the Senate’s last scheduled workday before the state work period beginning 08-10, which is why CLARITY is now dead until September.
