### Quantum / "Q-day" intersection — a real thread this week

- **2026-03-31 (historical context):** Google Quantum AI published research estimating Bitcoin's elliptic-curve signatures could be broken with **fewer than 500,000 physical qubits — roughly 20x fewer than 2019 estimates.**
- **~6.9M BTC (≈32% of circulating supply)** is exposed because the public key is already visible on-chain. *(Our internal quantum THESIS.md cites ~$718B in P2PK-held BTC — same phenomenon, older framing.)*
- AMINA's argument: BIP-110 and eCash have minimal support, but the **slowest-moving and most consequential upgrade is the post-quantum migration via BIP-360** — a multi-year programme that "has not properly started."

## Weekend Developments

**The Coldcard exploit is the live weekend tape, and it is still growing.**

| Timestamp | Reported loss | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-07-30, 01:31–01:56 UTC (25 min) | **594.48 BTC / ~$38.3M** from ~500 single-sig wallets | CoinDesk 2026-07-31 |
| 2026-08-01 | **1,082.65 BTC / ~$70.2M** (41-minute sweep) | The Hacker News 2026-08-01 |
| **2026-08-02 (current)** | **~1,367 BTC / ~$88.6M across 4,585 addresses, in three waves** | **Galaxy Research via Decrypt 2026-08-02** |

- **Root cause:** a **March 2021 build error in Coldcard firmware 4.0.0**. The `libngu` library checked whether a macro *existed* rather than whether it was *enabled*, binding the build to MicroPython's Yasmarang software PRNG instead of the STM32 hardware RNG. Result: seeds generated with far too little entropy, making private keys guessable **without physical access to the device**.
- **Blast radius:** Mk2/Mk3 firmware 4.0.0–4.1.9 confirmed vulnerable; Mk4/Mk5 (pre-5.6.0), Q (pre-1.5.0Q) and Edge builds also affected — Mk4/Q/Mk5 generate only **72-bit entropy**. Galaxy's assessment: **every single-sig Coldcard address generated after the March 2021 flaw will eventually be drained.**
- **Attacker profile:** Alex Thorn (Galaxy) called the sweeps "deliberate and programmatic, **probably orchestrated with a large language model**." Stolen coins averaged **3.18 years of dormancy** — the victims were long-term holders. **~600 suspected attacker addresses** have been reported to federal investigators and compliance firms. **Funds from all three waves remain parked and unmoved.**
- **Coinkite response:** emergency firmware released **2026-07-31**; users with exposed seeds told to regenerate on patched firmware and move coins, with 50+ fair dice rolls recommended as the specific mitigation.
- **Market consequence — this is the part that matters for Monday:** the exploit is **driving holders out of self-custody and back onto centralized exchanges and into ETFs**, inverting "not your keys, not your coins." CoinDesk's own framing (2026-07-31): the exploit "shakes faith in self-custody, may push investors to ETFs." Watch Monday's IBIT/FBTC prints for whether that shows up as flow.
